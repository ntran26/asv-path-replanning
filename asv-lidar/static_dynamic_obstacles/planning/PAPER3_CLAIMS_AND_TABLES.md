## Part 1. Claim ledger (restated 2026-11-05)

**Status:** restated on 2026-11-05 against the draft of that date
(`Paper3_Sections1-4_clean_rev05Nov.docx`), the results files and the two field plans. The
simulation claims (section 1) carry their evidence. The field claims (section 2) are
pre-registered here, their thresholds confirmed by the author on 2026-11-07, before calibration session 1. After
sign-off a claim may be weakened or withdrawn, but not added or moved to fit a result. The
earlier drafts (2026-09-18 to 2026-10-25, with claims C-2 to C-6 and tables R1 to R9 in full)
are in git history, last in commit 3b207bd07.

Every claim in the abstract, Section 6.1 and the conclusion maps to a row here; **Evidence**
names the table, figure or record that decides it.

**Central questions** (Section 1.3 of the draft, unlabelled there):
- **Q1:** does the deployed policy complete the navigation task with encounter-consistent
  behaviour, including in channels and among static obstacles where the room to manoeuvre is
  limited? (formerly RQ1)
- **Q2:** how does the deployed policy compare with the baselines trained or tuned on the same
  task: three further deep reinforcement learning learners under the same protocol, and two
  classical comparators? (restated 2026-11-23; formerly "which learner should be deployed, and
  which learner differences remain meaningful across training seeds and target behaviours?",
  RQ5)
- **Q3:** how does the deployed policy perform in physical basin trials? (replaces RQ4)

RQ2 (ablation), RQ3 (perception degradation) and RQ6 (width sweep) are dropped.

**Deployed-policy framing (decision 2026-11-23).** The paper reports the formulation and the
deployed policy first. Its results come first in Section 4.1, followed by one comparison table
against the baselines. The other learners are baselines, not a study of their own. The following
leave the main text for the appendices:
- the per-run table (Table 10);
- the learning curves of the four learners (Figure 5);
- the run histories;
- the account of recurrent PPO seed 1.

**Why SAC.** SAC is the learner of the authors' two earlier studies on the same vessel:
- **P1** (ICMCR 2026, doi 10.1109/ICMCR69541.2026.11533898): SAC 90.0-95.5 % against PPO
  49.4-67.4 % success over 1,000 randomised static-obstacle episodes, across five reward
  weightings. That study used raw LiDAR beams and rudder-only control.
- **P2** (Drones 2026, 10, 680, reference [13] of the draft): SAC chosen for continuous rudder and
  propulsion control, off-policy sample efficiency over randomised layouts, and entropy-driven
  exploration. Against a like-for-like PPO baseline it reached 92.4 % against 90.5 % (three seeds
  each, 500 episodes).

That evidence covers path following and static obstacles only, so it motivates the choice of
learner; it is not a result of this paper. TQC appears in neither study. Between SAC and TQC, the
registered rule decides (C-12), and the draft keeps saying that this rule used the test set.

**Contributions** (Section 1.3, in order): 1 the formulation; 2 the geometric framework for
constrained encounter responses; 3 the deployed policy (SAC, the learner of P1 and P2),
benchmarked against three further learners and two classical comparators under one protocol
(restated 2026-11-23; formerly "the deployed policy, chosen and benchmarked"); 4 the evaluation
design; 5 the physical encounter trials. Contribution 2 is a design component of the
formulation and carries no tested claim (its width thresholds are design reasoning, Section
2.2.4).

**Status values:** *supported* (the draft's evidence agrees with the claim as worded);
*prediction not met* (the claim holds but a registered threshold does not); *post hoc* (stated
from the results, with no prediction registered before them); *pending* (evidence not yet
collected); *withdrawn* or *dropped*, with the date.

### 3. What the paper will not claim

- Open-water Rule 15/17 role tables. The own ship gives way to a crossing target from either
  side (narrow-channel convention, A17), and the paper states this. No comparator keeps the
  open-water roles, so the effect of the convention is not measured.
- Rule 3(g) status for the own ship, Rule 17(a)(ii) or 17(b) action, Rule 18 responsibilities,
  Rule 19 restricted visibility, sound signals, or multi-target encounters.
- Legal compliance or certification. The behaviour is COLREGs-aware, not COLREGs-compliant, and
  no per-rule compliance rate is reported.
- That operating bounds establish the legal applicability of Rule 9, or that a channel width is
  a universal legal criterion for a narrow channel. Behaviour in channels narrower than the test
  set's is not established.
- That every dynamic encounter is avoidable from the policy's observations: the feasibility
  check uses the target's true motion.
- Robustness to perception poorer than nominal, or results with a sensor other than the 2D LiDAR
  (radar is a design scope of the interface, not a tested result).
- That the rule-related behaviour is learned rather than supplied: the classifier supplies the
  rule regime and no ablation separates the two.
- A ranking of SAC over TQC, or any learner difference below about 0.05.
- That the safety layer guarantees collision avoidance, raises success, or is a fully held-out
  result; its interventions are never credited to the policy.
- A field success rate comparable with the test set's, results for reactive, noncompliant or
  speed-changing targets on the water, or operation outside one calm-water basin.
- Full-scale performance beyond the Froude-similarity reading of Section 2.1.5.

### 4. Framing rules

- Strata are described by geometry only, never as "challenging for classical methods".
- Every learner result reports its seed spread; three seeds give a limited estimate of training
  variability.
- Checkpoints are selected on the validation set; the learner family is chosen on the test set,
  and the paper says so; the field trials are the independent evaluation of the deployed policy.
- Learned behaviour is reported with the safety layer off; the layer's results stand apart
  (Section 4.5, and in shadow mode in Section 5).
- Field runs: invalid runs are kept and reported as invalid; operator stops are counted apart
  from the policy's behaviour; the scenario list, metric definitions, analysis scripts and empty
  result tables are committed before the first trial session.
